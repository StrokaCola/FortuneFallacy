# FortuneFallacy2 — Boss Roster Illustration Brief

Briefing pack for the 8 final-trial anomalies. Hand directly to a contracted concept artist.

Authored: 2026-05-16. Canonical source data: [`src-next/data/blinds.ts`](../src-next/data/blinds.ts) `BOSS_BLINDS` + `BOSS_CINEMATIC_FLAVOR`.

## Shared Style Bible

Read this once. Every brief below assumes it.

- **Period reference**: scientific-astronomical illustration meets sci-fi paperback cover meets brutalist sculpture. Think the Voyager Golden Record diagrams scaled up and painted by Chesley Bonestell, then photographed under raking light. **No mysticism, no occult symbology, no tarot, no robed figures.** These are objects, not entities.
- **Format**: each boss is a portrait of a *celestial body* — a moon, a dwarf planet, a fragment. The body is the subject. If a humanoid form appears at all, it is mineral, mechanical, or implied by the topography — never a person in robes, never a face under a hood.
- **Silhouette test**: at 32×32px the outline alone must be identifiable. Pluto, Charon, and Phobos all read as "irregular sphere" in thumbnail — the silhouette differentiation has to come from proportion, surface scarring, and orientation, not pose.
- **Palette discipline**: each boss gets a primary accent (listed below). The plate is **80% palette** anchored on that accent + a deep cosmic violet `#1c1245`. The remaining 20% is one secondary metallic — gold leaf `#f5c451`, bone white `#f3f0ff`, or oxidized copper. **No third color** unless the brief explicitly names one.
- **Surface, not skin**: render every boss as terrain. Craters, fault lines, ice fissures, rust, fractured anorthosite. Where a form *appears* anthropomorphic, it should read as accidental — a mountain range that happens to resemble a profile, not a body that happens to be made of rock.
- **No faces, no eyes**: if a viewer wants to find an eye, let them find one in a crater shadow or a basin. Never paint one. Never paint a mouth. The threat is geological / orbital, not personal.
- **Aurora-violet underlight**: every plate carries a soft `#9577ff` rim glow from below or behind, mimicking the cosmos backdrop in-engine. Bake it in so the plate ships with its own atmospheric anchor.

## 1. Pluto — *The Demoted Gambler*

**Mechanic:** 1s refuse to transform. Phase 2: only even faces score. Cinematic line: *"the gambler's bones rattle."*

**Silhouette**
Asymmetric ovoid. A heart-shape impact basin (callback to the real Tombaugh Regio) carved into the upper half — but rotated so it reads as a *void* in the body, not a feature. Lower half visibly fractured: a long fault line splits the body roughly into two unequal halves.

**Motif**
A halo of small angular fragments orbiting the equator — dice-sized chunks, six visible. Each fragment is a different convex polyhedron (d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20 silhouettes, but rough-hewn from rock, not engraved with pips). One fragment is missing from the ring — the absent piece is the missing "1."

**Palette**
Accent `#44bb66` (jade — read as cold methane ice on the surface, not warm green). Body in graphite + bone white. Heart-basin interior in deepest cosmos `#1c1245`. Fragment ring tipped with faint gold leaf only where the missing piece *should* be — the gap glows where the others don't.

**Mood**
A celestial body that was reclassified and held a grudge about it. The threat is procedural — Pluto enforces a rule the player didn't agree to.

**Refs**
Real NASA New Horizons close-up of Pluto's Tombaugh Regio + Sputnik Planitia, Chesley Bonestell's *The Conquest of Space* dwarf planet plates, Anish Kapoor's *Cloud Gate* surface treatment (mirror-polished but cratered).

**Avoid**
Anthropomorphic gambler with dice. Pluto is the *table*, not the dealer. No hands. No hood.

## 2. Ceres — *The Belt-Bound Mother*

**Mechanic:** Hand capped at 4. Phase 2: consumables sealed. Cinematic line: *"the belt closes around you."*

**Silhouette**
Compact sphere, smaller than the others (Ceres is the smallest dwarf planet — honor that). Wrapped *horizontally* by a dense band of asteroid fragments at the equator. The band is so packed it nearly doubles the body's apparent width.

**Motif**
The asteroid belt as a literal *belt* of debris. Five named fragments stand out from the crowd, each a hand-rendered chunk with distinct silhouette. A noticeable gap separates fragment four from where fragment five *would* be — the gap is the visual key to "capped at 4."

**Palette**
Accent `#ffaa44` (harvest orange — read as iron-rich ferrous oxide on a dry rocky body). Sphere in warm bone with copper-oxide shadows. Asteroid fragments: oxidized copper inside, dim gold leaf on the named five. Background cosmos.

**Mood**
A small body that became a fortress. Ceres withholds because it's surrounded — there is no malice, only the perimeter.

**Refs**
NASA Dawn mission surface imagery of Ceres (Occator Crater), Hayao Miyazaki's *Castle in the Sky* floating debris fields (the rock-not-magic version), Brutalist precast concrete with embedded aggregate (Tadao Andō's *Church of the Light* surface texture).

**Avoid**
Maternal figure with arms around a planet. The "Belt-Bound *Mother*" is metaphor — the belt is the mother, the planet is the child. No woman, no fabric, no robe.

## 3. Triton — *The Single Flyby*

**Mechanic:** No rerolls. Phase 2: hand capped at 4. Cinematic line: *"the flyby begins."*

**Silhouette**
Horizontally elongated. A moon stretched by motion-blur into a comet-like form — leading edge a hard-rendered hemisphere, trailing edge dissolving into a streak of frozen nitrogen geysers. The silhouette reads as "a body in transit," not "a body at rest."

**Motif**
A single tally notch carved into the leading hemisphere — one mark, gold-leafed, the only inscribed element on the entire plate. Because Triton passes once. The notch is the player's only window.

**Palette**
Accent `#aa6644` (oxidized copper / dim rust — read as cryovolcanic mineral deposit). Body in pale graphite. Trailing geysers in faint violet glow against deep cosmos. The notch in gold leaf. **One** spot of pure bone white at the leading-edge sunlit cap.

**Mood**
Pure velocity. No personality — only trajectory. The player feels watched by a *direction*.

**Refs**
Voyager 2's actual Triton imagery (south polar terrain, dark streaks from geysers), Robert McCall's NASA mission paintings (the "spacecraft passing a moon" compositions), the leading edge of a comet rendered by Aristarkh Lentulov (Russian Cubo-Futurist).

**Avoid**
A god of the sea with a trident. Triton-the-moon is named for the god but is unrelated. No water, no spear, no anthropomorphism.

## 4. Phobos — *Decaying Orbit*

**Mechanic:** Locks release on roll. Phase 2: consumables sealed. Cinematic line: *"orbit decays · the chain rusts."*

**Silhouette**
Irregular potato (Phobos is famously not spheroidal — honor that). Off-center, tilted at an aggressive angle as if mid-fall. The Stickney Crater (real feature) dominates one face and is rendered nearly half the body's diameter.

**Motif**
A series of parallel grooves (the real Phobos has them — likely from Stickney's impact) crossing the body diagonally. Each groove is rendered as a small chain of broken links — gold leaf at the body's surface, rusting and falling away into the void at the trailing end. The chain literally rusts as it leaves the moon.

**Palette**
Accent `#cc2244` (crimson — read as iron oxide / Martian-system kinship, not warning red). Body in blackened steel + deep cosmos shadow. Stickney Crater interior glows faintly aurora-violet from below. Chain segments: gold at root, rust at tip. No bone white.

**Mood**
Falling toward Mars in slow motion. Phobos is real and is actually decaying — the brief is reality with the timeline compressed. The player feels gravity, not menace.

**Refs**
Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter's Phobos imagery (the grooves are visible), Donald Davis's painting *Phobos and Mars* (NASA archive), broken architectural ironwork in Piranesi's *Carceri d'Invenzione*.

**Avoid**
A war god in a helmet. Phobos-the-moon is named for the personification of fear but is *unrelated*. No face. No helmet. No personification.

## 5. Callisto — *Cratered Silence*

**Mechanic:** Catalysts inert. Phase 2 *relaxes* — silence lifts at half-target. Cinematic line: *"the cratered silence opens."*

**Silhouette**
Heavily pockmarked sphere. Callisto is the most cratered body in the solar system — make the surface noise the silhouette's defining quality. The body's outline at thumbnail size should read as "cratered" by silhouette alone (small notches in the visible rim).

**Motif**
One enormous crater — the real Callisto has a feature called *Valhalla*, a multi-ring impact basin. Render it dominating the visible face. Inside the central peak of Valhalla, a single shaft of aurora-violet light emerges in the *phase-2 variant only*. In the base plate, the central peak is dark.

**Palette**
Accent `#aa66ff` (deep violet — Callisto owns the violet end of the palette). Surface in graphite + ice-white with violet shadow in every crater. Gold leaf on the rim of Valhalla *only* — every other crater is unornamented. No bone white anywhere else.

**Mood**
Watchful geology. Callisto has been hit too many times to register a new impact — the "silence" is overload, not absence. When she relaxes, it's because something finally got through.

**Refs**
Galileo spacecraft imagery of Callisto's actual Valhalla basin, Olafur Eliasson's *Big Bang Fountain* (frozen-instant of impact), the surface of the moon photographed during a partial lunar eclipse.

**Avoid**
A stacked-stone monument or face on the surface. The point is *one* crater that opens, surrounded by ten thousand that don't. Resist the urge to make the surface read as a face.

## 6. Eris — *The Unmaker Arrives First*

**Mechanic:** Catalysts inert on first 2 hands. Phase 2 swaps to a different threat (mod squeeze). Cinematic line: *"the unmaker arrives first."*

**Silhouette**
Sphere, but visibly *cleaved* — a clean cut runs from north pole to south pole, splitting the body into two slightly-misaligned halves. The cut is sharp, geological, not violent.

**Motif**
The cleave itself. Where the two halves meet, the gap glows faintly aurora-violet. Two small numerical fragments sit embedded in the cleave like wedges holding the body apart — a "2" on one side, a "3" on the other (callback to the Eris Apple easter egg, the all-prime hand). Gold leaf on the numerals only.

**Palette**
Accent `#ff7847` (ember — read as primordial heat venting through the split). Body surface in cooled iron / cold graphite. Cleave interior glows ember at the seam, fading to aurora-violet deeper in. No bone white.

**Mood**
A dwarf planet that arrived already broken — Eris is named for the discord that demoted Pluto. The "unmaker" doesn't act on you; the unmaker IS what already happened. The player walks into a system already off its hinges.

**Refs**
The Hubble imagery of Eris and its moon Dysnomia (the actual discovered body), Andy Goldsworthy's split-stone earthworks, the cooling-fracture pattern in basalt columns at the Giant's Causeway.

**Avoid**
A literal apple. The Eris Apple is the easter egg's name, not the boss's costume. The motif is *two embedded numerals in a cleave* — no fruit.

## 7. Charon — *The Ferryman's Tariff*

**Mechanic:** Consumables sealed. Phase 2: no transforms on 1s. Cinematic line: *"the ferryman holds your shards."*

**Silhouette**
Sphere, but distinctively bisected by a horizontal canyon — the real Charon has a massive equatorial chasm system (Argo Chasma). Render it as a dark band slicing across the body's middle, three to four times the depth proportionally exaggerated from the real moon.

**Motif**
Along the canyon's length, embedded at regular intervals, sit small mechanical safe-deposit boxes — three to five visible, each padlocked with gold-leaf hardware. They are *built into* the canyon, not laid on top. The locks are the visual key to "consumables sealed."

**Palette**
Accent `#8a8aaa` (cold grey — Charon is the most desaturated of the eight). Surface in graphite with violet shadow in the chasm. Each box in a different muted secondary (oxidized copper, bone, dim gold) so the row reads as "all your colors, locked away inside the rock."

**Mood**
A moon that became a vault. Charon orbits Pluto in a mutual tidal-lock — the two bodies stare at each other forever. Here Charon has decided to hold your things. The vault is older than you. Bureaucratic patience.

**Refs**
New Horizons imagery of Charon's actual Serenity Chasma and Argo Chasma (the equatorial canyon is real and dramatic), Brutalist bank vault architecture (the John Hancock Center's mechanical aesthetic), the half-buried machinery in *Annihilation* (the film).

**Avoid**
A hooded boatman with a pole. Charon-the-moon is named for the boatman but is unrelated. No boat. No pole. No person.

## 8. Sedna — *The Widening Slot*

**Mechanic:** Mod slots capped at 1. Phase 2: no transforms on 1s. Cinematic line: *"the slot is wider than you remember."*

**Silhouette**
Elongated, dim, distant — Sedna is one of the most distant known objects in the solar system. Render it as a body that *recedes* into the frame. The silhouette is slightly tapered (the far edge less defined than the near), as if the body is being seen through depth haze.

**Motif**
A single rectangular slot carved into the body's surface — proportionally large, like a dock opening on the side of an asteroid. The slot is dark, lit faintly from within by aurora-violet. Hairline fractures radiate outward from the slot's edges, suggesting it's *widening* as you watch. No second slot anywhere on the body.

**Palette**
Accent `#4477cc` (cold deep blue — Sedna is the most reddish object in the solar system in reality, but the game lore wants cold-blue ownership; honor the lore). Surface in graphite with violet undertones — *no warm colors at all*. Slot interior glows faint aurora. Frost-line tracery in faint bone white only where the fractures spread.

**Mood**
Distance made physical. Sedna doesn't approach — she recedes, and you can never quite catch up. The slot is your only access point, and it's getting harder to use, not easier. Calm inevitability.

**Refs**
The Hubble + ALMA composite imagery of the actual Sedna (a very small reddish dot — use it for scale-anchor reference only), Roger Dean's *Yes* album covers (cold blue distant worlds), the empty docking bays in Stanisław Lem's *Solaris* (the novel, not the film).

**Avoid**
A submerged figure, a woman, hair flowing, water. Sedna-the-body is named for the Inuit sea figure but is unrelated. No water imagery whatsoever.

## Delivery Checklist

Hand back the following per boss:

1. **Hero plate** — 1024×1536 PNG, full color, aurora-violet rim baked in. RGB sRGB, transparent background.
2. **Reveal plate** — 512×768 PNG, identical art, exported at the smaller size with anti-aliasing pre-baked (no downsampling artifacts at 180×270 in-engine).
3. **Thumbnail variant** — 128×128 PNG, recomposed so the silhouette reads at this size. May crop tighter than the hero — favor the focal motif at thumbnail scale.
4. **Silhouette test** — 128×128 PNG, pure black on transparent, of just the boss outline. We use this to QA the silhouette discipline in the style bible.
5. **Phase-2 variant** (Callisto + Pluto + Eris only) — same dimensions as Hero plate, with the phase-2 visual change applied (Callisto's Valhalla central peak lit, Pluto's even-fragment ring highlighted, Eris's cleave actively venting). The other 5 bosses ship one plate each.
6. **Source files** — layered PSD or PROCREATE per boss, named `{bossId}-source.{ext}`. Required for future palette swaps when cosmetic skins ship.

Naming convention: `boss-{id}-{variant}.png` → `boss-pluto-hero.png`, `boss-pluto-reveal.png`, `boss-pluto-thumb.png`, `boss-pluto-silhouette.png`, `boss-pluto-phase2.png`.

Style approval: deliver Pluto and Charon **first** as the calibration pair (extremes of the palette: jade green vs. cold grey). Get sign-off on those two before producing the remaining six.

**Timeline target:** 2 weeks for calibration pair, 4–6 weeks for full set.
