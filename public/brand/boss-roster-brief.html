Eight Anomalies, One Sky.

Concept-art handoff for the eight Final-Trial boss plates of FortuneFallacy2. Each anomaly is a portrait of a celestial body — not a character. Surface, not skin. Geology, not personification.

00 · shared style bible

Read this once. Every brief below assumes it.

These are objects, not entities. The brief is intentionally hostile to mystical or anthropomorphic interpretation — if the artist's instinct is to draw a figure, route to a different artist.

Rule 01

Period reference

Scientific-astronomical illustration × sci-fi paperback × brutalist sculpture. Think Voyager Golden Record scaled up and painted by Chesley Bonestell, photographed under raking light. No mysticism, no occult symbology, no tarot, no robed figures.
Rule 02

Format · the body is the subject

Each boss is a portrait of a celestial body — moon, dwarf planet, fragment. If a humanoid form appears, it is mineral, mechanical, or implied by topography — never a person in robes, never a face under a hood.
Rule 03

Silhouette test · 32×32

At thumbnail size the outline alone must be identifiable. Pluto, Charon, Phobos all read as "irregular sphere" — differentiation comes from proportion, surface scarring, orientation, not pose.
Rule 04

Palette discipline · 80/20

Each boss owns a primary accent. Plate is 80% palette on that accent + deep cosmic violet #1c1245. Remaining 20% is one secondary metallic — gold leaf, bone white, or oxidized copper. No third color unless named.
Rule 05

Surface, not skin · no faces

Render every boss as terrain. Craters, fault lines, ice fissures, rust, fractured anorthosite. If a viewer wants an eye, let them find one in a crater shadow. Never paint one. Never paint a mouth.
Rule 06

Aurora-violet underlight

Every plate carries a soft #9577ff rim glow from below or behind, mimicking the cosmos backdrop in-engine. Bake it in — the plate ships with its own atmospheric anchor.

Silhouette

Asymmetric ovoid. A heart-shape impact basin (Tombaugh Regio) carved into the upper half — but rotated so it reads as a void in the body, not a feature. Lower half visibly fractured: a long fault line splits the body roughly into two unequal halves.

Motif

A halo of small angular fragments orbiting the equator — dice-sized chunks, six visible. Each is a different convex polyhedron (d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, d20 silhouettes, but rough-hewn rock, not engraved with pips). One fragment is missing from the ring — the absent piece is the missing "1."

Palette · 80/20
Mood

A celestial body that was reclassified and held a grudge about it. The threat is procedural — Pluto enforces a rule the player didn't agree to. The plate is the table, not the dealer.

Refs
  • NASA New Horizons close-up · Tombaugh Regio + Sputnik Planitia
  • Chesley Bonestell, The Conquest of Space · dwarf planet plates
  • Anish Kapoor, Cloud Gate · mirror-polished but cratered surface
Avoid
  • Anthropomorphic gambler with dice
  • Hands. Hoods. A dealer figure of any kind
  • Dice engraved with pips — fragments are rough rock

Silhouette

Compact sphere, smaller than the others — Ceres is the smallest dwarf planet, honor that. Wrapped horizontally by a dense band of asteroid fragments at the equator. The band is so packed it nearly doubles the body's apparent width.

Motif

The asteroid belt as a literal belt of debris. Five named fragments stand out from the crowd, each a hand-rendered chunk with distinct silhouette. A noticeable gap separates fragment four from where fragment five would be — the gap is the visual key to "capped at 4."

Palette · 80/20
Mood

A small body that became a fortress. Ceres withholds because it's surrounded — there is no malice, only the perimeter. The belt is the mother. The planet is the child.

Refs
  • NASA Dawn mission · Occator Crater surface imagery
  • Miyazaki, Castle in the Sky · floating debris fields (rock, not magic)
  • Tadao Andō, Church of the Light · brutalist precast aggregate
Avoid
  • Maternal figure with arms around a planet
  • A woman. Fabric. A robe
  • "Mother" rendered literally — the metaphor is the belt

Silhouette

Horizontally elongated. A moon stretched by motion-blur into a comet-like form — leading edge a hard-rendered hemisphere, trailing edge dissolving into a streak of frozen nitrogen geysers. The silhouette reads as "a body in transit," not "a body at rest."

Motif

A single tally notch carved into the leading hemisphere — one mark, gold-leafed, the only inscribed element on the entire plate. Because Triton passes once. The notch is the player's only window.

Palette · 80/20
Mood

Pure velocity. No personality — only trajectory. The player feels watched by a direction.

Refs

Silhouette

Irregular potato — Phobos is famously not spheroidal, honor that. Off-center, tilted at an aggressive angle as if mid-fall. The Stickney Crater dominates one face and is rendered nearly half the body's diameter.

Motif

Parallel grooves crossing the body diagonally — each groove rendered as a small chain of broken links. Gold leaf at the body's surface, rusting and falling away into the void at the trailing end. The chain literally rusts as it leaves the moon.

Palette · 80/20
Mood

Falling toward Mars in slow motion. Phobos is real and is actually decaying — the brief is reality with the timeline compressed. The player feels gravity, not menace.

Refs
  • Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter · Phobos grooves are visible
  • Donald Davis, Phobos and Mars · NASA archive
  • Piranesi, Carceri d'Invenzione · broken architectural ironwork
Avoid
  • A war god in a helmet
  • A face. A helmet. Personification of any kind
  • "Warning red" — this is iron oxide, not a stoplight

Silhouette

Heavily pockmarked sphere. Callisto is the most cratered body in the solar system — make the surface noise the silhouette's defining quality. At thumbnail size the body's outline should read "cratered" by silhouette alone (small notches in the visible rim).

Motif

One enormous crater — Valhalla, a multi-ring impact basin — dominates the visible face. Inside the central peak: a single shaft of aurora-violet light emerges in the phase-2 variant only. In the base plate, the central peak is dark.

Palette · 80/20
Mood

Watchful geology. Callisto has been hit too many times to register a new impact — the "silence" is overload, not absence. When she relaxes, it's because something finally got through.

Refs
  • Galileo spacecraft · Callisto's actual Valhalla basin
  • Olafur Eliasson, Big Bang Fountain · frozen instant
  • The moon during partial lunar eclipse
Avoid
  • A face on the surface — pareidolia is the enemy
  • Stacked-stone monument or shrine
  • More than one "opening" crater — Valhalla stands alone

Silhouette

Sphere, but visibly cleaved — a clean cut runs from north pole to south pole, splitting the body into two slightly-misaligned halves. The cut is sharp, geological, not violent.

Motif

The cleave itself. Where the two halves meet, the gap glows faintly aurora-violet. Two small numerical fragments sit embedded in the cleave like wedges holding the body apart — a "2" on one side, a "3" on the other (Eris Apple easter egg, the all-prime hand). Gold leaf on the numerals only.

Palette · 80/20
Mood

A dwarf planet that arrived already broken. The unmaker doesn't act on you — the unmaker is what already happened. The player walks into a system already off its hinges.

Refs
  • Hubble · Eris and its moon Dysnomia
  • Andy Goldsworthy · split-stone earthworks
  • Giant's Causeway · cooling-fracture basalt columns
Avoid
  • A literal apple
  • "Discord" rendered as conflict — this is geology
  • A figure tossing anything anywhere

Silhouette

Sphere, but distinctively bisected by a horizontal canyon — Charon's real Argo Chasma. Render it as a dark band slicing across the body's middle, three to four times the depth proportionally exaggerated from the real moon.

Motif

Along the canyon's length, at regular intervals, sit small mechanical safe-deposit boxes — three to five visible, each padlocked with gold-leaf hardware. They are built into the canyon, not laid on top. The locks are the visual key to "consumables sealed."

Palette · 80/20
Mood

A moon that became a vault. Charon and Pluto are mutually tidal-locked — they stare at each other forever. Here Charon has decided to hold your things. The vault is older than you. Bureaucratic patience.

Refs
  • New Horizons · Charon's Serenity + Argo Chasma

  • Annihilation (film) · half-buried machinery in landscape

Silhouette

Elongated, dim, distant — Sedna is one of the most distant known objects in the solar system. Render it as a body that recedes into the frame. Silhouette slightly tapered (far edge less defined than the near), as if seen through depth haze.

Motif

A single rectangular slot carved into the body's surface — proportionally large, like a dock opening on the side of an asteroid. Slot lit faintly from within by aurora-violet. Hairline fractures radiate outward from the slot's edges, suggesting it's widening as you watch. No second slot anywhere on the body.

Palette · 80/20

Lore override: Sedna is reddish in reality. Honor the lore — cold blue owns this plate. No warm colors anywhere.

Mood

Distance made physical. Sedna doesn't approach — she recedes, and you can never quite catch up. The slot is your only access point, and it's getting harder to use, not easier. Calm inevitability.

Refs
  • Hubble + ALMA composite · the actual Sedna (scale anchor)
  • Roger Dean · Yes album covers · cold blue distant worlds
  • Lem, Solaris (the novel) · empty docking bays
Avoid
  • A submerged figure. A woman. Hair flowing. Water
  • Any water imagery whatsoever
  • A second slot — the slot is alone
